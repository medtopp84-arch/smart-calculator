Trench Volume Calculator: Excavation, Soil Swell and Truck Loads

This calculator finds the volume of soil to be excavated for a trench, the larger volume that soil occupies once it is loosened, and the number of dump truck trips needed to haul it away. It is useful for pipeline and cable trenches, drainage lines, footings and utility work, and for estimating equipment hire and backfill.

How the calculation works

A straight trench with vertical walls is a rectangular prism, so its volume is length times width times depth. Soil in the ground (in-situ or "bank" volume) is packed. Once dug it takes up more space because of air between the particles. This increase is the swell factor, and it decides how many trucks you need.

In-situ volume V = Length × Width × Depth
Loose volume = V × (1 + Swell % ÷ 100)
Truck trips = Loose volume ÷ Truck capacity (rounded up)

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the trench length, width and depth in meters.
  2. Choose a soil swell factor that matches your ground.
  3. Enter the capacity of your dump truck in cubic meters.
  4. Read the in-situ volume, the loose volume and the number of trips.

Worked example 1

A water pipe trench is 100 m long, 1.2 m wide and 1.5 m deep, in standard earth with 25 % swell, using 15 m³ trucks.

  • In-situ volume: 100 × 1.2 × 1.5 = 180 m³
  • Loose volume: 180 × 1.25 = 225 m³
  • Truck trips: 225 ÷ 15 = 15 trips

Worked example 2

A drainage trench is 250 m long, 0.8 m wide and 1.2 m deep in compact clay with 15 % swell, using 12 m³ trucks.

  • In-situ volume: 250 × 0.8 × 1.2 = 240 m³
  • Loose volume: 240 × 1.15 = 276 m³
  • Truck trips: 276 ÷ 12 = 23, so 23 trips

Swell depends on the soil type, moisture and how it is dug. The values in the calculator are typical planning figures. For a real project use the value from your geotechnical report or your contractor's experience.

Limits of this calculator

  • Vertical walls only. It assumes a rectangular cross-section. Trenches with sloped (battered) sides need more excavation. For a symmetric trapezoid, use the average width: bottom width plus the depth times the side slope ratio.
  • Constant depth. If the depth changes along the route, split the trench into sections and add the volumes.
  • No pipe or bedding deduction. The result is the total excavated volume, not the volume of backfill needed. Subtract the pipe volume and the bedding layer when ordering fill.

Safety note

Trench collapse is a leading cause of fatal accidents in excavation work. Many safety regulations, including OSHA rules in the United States, require sloping, shoring or a trench box for trenches from about 1.5 m (5 ft) deep, and sometimes for shallower ones depending on the soil. Always follow your local regulations and the instructions of a competent person on site.

Common mistakes

  • Sizing trucks from in-situ volume. Trucks carry loose soil, so use the loose volume.
  • Forgetting extra width for working space. The trench must often be wider than the pipe by a clearance on each side.
  • Rounding trips down. A partly filled last truck still counts as a trip. The calculator rounds up.
  • Mixing units. All dimensions are in meters and the truck capacity in cubic meters.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between in-situ and loose volume?

In-situ volume is the soil measured in place in the ground. Loose volume is the same soil after it has been dug and has expanded. Loose volume is always larger.

Which volume do I pay or bill for?

Excavation is often measured and paid by in-situ (bank) volume, while hauling is planned by loose volume. Check your contract.

How many cubic meters does a dump truck carry?

It varies widely, from about 6 m³ for small tippers to 20 m³ or more for large trucks. Use the rated capacity of the truck you will actually use, and remember that weight limits may be reached before the body is full, especially with wet soil or rock.

Does the result include compaction of backfill?

No. Backfilled soil is usually compacted to a volume close to or slightly below its original in-situ volume, and imported fill is a separate calculation. See our disclaimer about using results for final design.
