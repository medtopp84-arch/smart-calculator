Slope Calculator: Elevation Difference, Percent Grade & Angle

This online slope calculator allows you to quickly calculate the elevation difference, gradient percentage, and slope angle between two distinct spatial points using their elevation values and horizontal separation distance. It provides highly reliable computations required in land surveying, highway engineering, stormwater management, underground piping placement, and topographic landscape architecture.

What is Slope in Engineering and Surveying?

Slope—frequently designated as grade, incline, or pitch—quantifies the vertical rise or fall of terrain relative to its horizontal run distance. Gradients can be expressed as a percent grade, an angle in degrees, or a proportional ratio (e.g., 1:12). Regardless of the unit chosen, all three representations mathematically define identical spatial steepness.

Δh = | Elevation A − Elevation B |
Slope (%) = (Δh ÷ Horizontal Distance) × 100
Angle (°) = arctan(Δh ÷ Horizontal Distance) × (180 ÷ π)
Slope Ratio = 1 : (Horizontal Distance ÷ Δh)

How to Calculate Slope Step-by-Step

  1. Select your preferred unit system (meters or feet). Ensure all inputs maintain matching units.
  2. Enter the absolute vertical elevations for both Point A and Point B.
  3. Input the true horizontal distance separating the two locations (do not use direct surface distance along the slope).
  4. Review the automatically calculated vertical elevation drop/rise, grade percentage, and incline angle.

Practical Worked Calculation Example

Suppose Point A stands at an altitude of 150 meters, Point B sits at 111 meters, and their horizontal distance is 500 meters:

  • Elevation Difference (Δh): |150 m − 111 m| = 39 m
  • Percent Slope: (39 ÷ 500) × 100 = 7.8 %
  • Slope Angle: arctan(0.078) ≈ 4.46°
  • Gradient Ratio: 500 ÷ 39 ≈ 1 : 12.8

Interpretation: The surface drops 7.8 meters vertically for every 100 meters traversed horizontally.

Understanding Percent Slope vs. Incline Angle Degrees

A common misconception is treating percent slope as identical to degrees. A 100% slope corresponds to a 45° angle (where rise equals run), rather than a vertical 90° angle. While percentages and degrees track closely on minor gradients, they diverge significantly as steepness grows:

  • 10% Slope: ≈ 5.71°
  • 50% Slope: ≈ 26.57°
  • 100% Slope: = 45.00°

Frequent Calculation Errors to Avoid

  • Confusing Slope Distance with Horizontal Distance: If your field measurement was taken directly along the sloped ground surface, convert it to horizontal distance prior to slope evaluation using our Angle and Offset Calculator.
  • Inconsistent Unit Usage: Combining elevation in feet with horizontal distance measured in meters yields invalid results.
  • Interchanging Percentage and Degrees: Always verify whether your project documentation requests gradient in percent or angular degrees.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I convert slope percentage directly into degrees?

Divide the percent value by 100, then compute the inverse tangent (arctan) of that decimal. For instance, a 7.8% slope translates to arctan(0.078) ≈ 4.46°.

What does a 1:12 slope ratio signify?

It indicates a 1-unit vertical elevation change for every 12 units of horizontal distance, equivalent to an 8.33% grade.

Can I use Imperial measurement units like feet or inches?

Yes. Select 'Feet' in the unit dropdown. Slope percentage and angle remain completely dimensionless and unaffected by unit choice.

The underlying trigonometric formulas are mathematically precise. However, official engineering construction plans must always be reviewed against professional survey datasets and regional building codes. Read our full disclaimer.
