Ideal Gas Law & Thermodynamics

The Ideal Gas Law is one of the most fundamental equations in thermodynamics. It describes the relationship between pressure (P), volume (V), temperature (T), and the number of moles (n) of an ideal gas. It is derived from the combination of Boyle's, Charles's, and Avogadro's laws.

P × V = n × R × T

Where:

  • P = Pressure (atm, Pa, mmHg, bar, psi)
  • V = Volume (L, m³, mL)
  • n = Number of moles of gas
  • R = Ideal gas constant (0.0821 L·atm/mol·K)
  • T = Temperature in Kelvin (K = °C + 273.15)

The Ideal Gas Law assumes that gas particles have negligible volume and no intermolecular forces. It works well for most gases under normal conditions (low pressure, high temperature). For real gases, use the Van der Waals equation.
