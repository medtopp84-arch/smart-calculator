Steel Pipe Weight Calculator: kg per Meter from OD and Wall Thickness

This calculator gives the weight of a round steel pipe from its outside diameter, wall thickness and length. It returns the weight per meter, the total weight in kilograms and tonnes, and the inner diameter. It is useful for estimating lifting loads, transport and trucking, pipe supports and hangers, material takeoff and cost estimates. You can choose carbon steel, stainless steel or aluminium.

The formula

The wall of a pipe is a ring. Its cross-sectional area is π × WT × (OD − WT), where OD is the outside diameter and WT the wall thickness, both in millimeters. Multiplying by the density of the metal gives the weight per meter. For carbon steel with a density of 7850 kg/m³ the constants combine into the well-known factor 0.02466.

Weight per meter (kg/m) = π × WT × (OD − WT) × ρ ÷ 1,000,000
Carbon steel (ρ = 7850): kg/m = 0.02466 × WT × (OD − WT)
Total weight = kg/m × Length
Inner diameter ID = OD − 2 × WT

OD and WT are in millimeters, ρ is the density in kg/m³ and the length is in meters.

How to use the calculator

  1. Choose the material (carbon steel, stainless steel or aluminium).
  2. Enter the outside diameter in millimeters.
  3. Enter the wall thickness in millimeters.
  4. Enter the total length in meters.
  5. Read the weight per meter, the total weight and the inner diameter.

Worked example 1: 8 inch pipe

A carbon steel pipe with an outside diameter of 219.1 mm, a wall thickness of 8.18 mm and a length of 12 m.

  • Weight per meter: 0.02466 × 8.18 × (219.1 − 8.18) = 42.55 kg/m
  • Total weight: 42.55 × 12 = 510.6 kg
  • Inner diameter: 219.1 − 2 × 8.18 = 202.74 mm

Worked example 2: 4 inch pipe, 100 m

A carbon steel pipe with an outside diameter of 114.3 mm and a wall thickness of 6.02 mm, 100 m in total.

  • Weight per meter: 0.02466 × 6.02 × (114.3 − 6.02) = 16.07 kg/m
  • Total weight: 16.07 × 100 = 1607 kg (about 1.61 tonnes)

Worked example 3: 20 inch pipeline

A carbon steel pipe with an outside diameter of 508 mm, a wall thickness of 9.53 mm and a length of 1000 m.

  • Weight per meter: 0.02466 × 9.53 × (508 − 9.53) = 117.15 kg/m
  • Total weight: 117,145 kg, about 117 tonnes

What this weight does and does not include

  • Empty bare pipe only. Coatings, insulation, linings, welds, flanges, fittings and supports are not included.
  • Nominal dimensions. Real pipes have manufacturing tolerances on wall thickness and mass, so the actual weight can differ a little from the calculation.
  • Contents not included. To add the weight of water or oil inside the pipe, use our Pipeline Volume and Hydrotest calculator and add the two masses.
  • Seamless and welded pipe. The formula covers the wall of a plain round pipe. Weld reinforcement is ignored.

Common mistakes

  • Using the nominal size instead of the outside diameter. A "6 inch" pipe has an outside diameter of 168.3 mm, not 152 mm or 150 mm.
  • Entering the wall thickness in centimeters or inches. Both diameter and thickness must be in millimeters.
  • Confusing WT with schedule. A schedule number is not a thickness. The thickness depends on the schedule and the nominal size.
  • Applying the 0.02466 factor to other metals. That constant is for carbon steel only. Choose the material in the calculator instead.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the weight of a steel pipe?

Multiply 0.02466 by the wall thickness and by the difference between outside diameter and wall thickness, all in millimeters. The result is the weight in kg per meter.

Where does 0.02466 come from?

It is π × 7850 ÷ 1,000,000, which converts the steel density and the ring area in square millimeters into kilograms per meter.

How do I convert kg/m to lb/ft?

Multiply by 0.6720. For example, 42.55 kg/m is about 28.6 lb/ft.

Can I use it for hollow sections?

No. It is for round pipes. Square and rectangular hollow sections need a different area formula. See our disclaimer about using results for final design and lifting.
