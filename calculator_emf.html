Electromotive Force (EMF) & Galvanic Cells

The electromotive force (EMF) is the maximum potential difference between two electrodes of a galvanic or voltaic cell. It represents the ability of an electrochemical cell to drive the flow of electrons through an external circuit, generating electrical energy from chemical reactions.

EMF = Ecathode − Eanode

Where:

  • Ecathode = Reduction potential of the cathode (higher value, in volts)
  • Eanode = Reduction potential of the anode (lower value, in volts)
  • EMF > 0 = Spontaneous reaction (galvanic cell)
  • EMF < 0 = Non-spontaneous reaction (electrolytic cell)

The standard electrode potentials (E⁰) are measured under standard conditions (25°C, 1 M concentration, 1 atm pressure). Values are relative to the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) with E⁰ = 0.00 V.
