Angle and Offset Calculator: Vertical Offset and Slope Distance

This calculator converts a horizontal distance and a slope angle into two values: the vertical offset (how much the line rises or falls) and the slope distance (the true length measured along the incline). It is useful for pipe routing, ramp and stair layout, roof and embankment work, and any survey where an angle is measured instead of two elevations.

How it works

The horizontal distance, the vertical offset and the slope distance form a right triangle. The horizontal distance is the side next to the angle (adjacent), the vertical offset is the side opposite it, and the slope distance is the hypotenuse.

Vertical offset = L × tan(θ)
Slope distance = L ÷ cos(θ)

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the horizontal distance L in meters.
  2. Enter the slope angle θ in degrees, between 0 and 90 (exclusive).
  3. Read the vertical offset and the actual slope distance.

Worked example 1

A pipeline runs 500 m horizontally on a 5° incline.

  • Vertical offset: 500 × tan(5°) = 500 × 0.08749 = 43.74 m
  • Slope distance: 500 ÷ cos(5°) = 500 ÷ 0.99619 = 501.91 m

The pipe is about 1.9 m longer than the horizontal distance, which matters when ordering material.

Worked example 2

A ramp covers 120 m horizontally at 12°.

  • Vertical offset: 120 × tan(12°) = 25.51 m
  • Slope distance: 120 ÷ cos(12°) = 122.68 m

On gentle slopes the slope distance is almost equal to the horizontal distance. The difference grows quickly above 15°.

If you measured the slope distance instead

Tape and laser measurements are often taken along the incline. In that case use the reverse relationships:

Horizontal distance = S × cos(θ)
Vertical offset = S × sin(θ)

where S is the measured slope distance. Once you have the horizontal distance and the offset, our Slope Calculator gives the percent grade.

Common mistakes

  • Calculator set to radians. This tool takes degrees. If you use a handheld calculator to check, make sure it is in degree mode.
  • Mixing up horizontal and slope distance. L here is always the horizontal distance.
  • Entering a percent grade as an angle. A 10 % grade is about 5.7°, not 10°.
  • Using angles near 90°. The tangent grows without limit, so results become meaningless. The tool accepts angles below 90° only.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between vertical offset and elevation difference?

They are the same quantity. "Offset" is the term often used in layout and pipe fitting, while "elevation difference" is used in surveying.

Why is slope distance always longer than horizontal distance?

It is the hypotenuse of a right triangle, which is always longer than either of the other two sides.

Can I enter a negative angle?

The tool is designed for 0° to 90°. For a downward slope, enter the positive angle and treat the offset as a drop.

Does this include the curvature of the earth?

No. It assumes a flat right triangle, which is accurate for the distances used in most construction work. See our disclaimer.
