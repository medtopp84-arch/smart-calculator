Combined Gas Law

The Combined Gas Law combines Boyle's Law, Charles's Law, and Gay-Lussac's Law into a single equation. It describes the relationship between pressure, volume, and temperature when the number of moles is constant.

(P₁ × V₁) / T₁ = (P₂ × V₂) / T₂

Where:

  • P₁, P₂ = Initial and final pressure
  • V₁, V₂ = Initial and final volume
  • T₁, T₂ = Initial and final temperature (in Kelvin)

The Combined Gas Law is used when pressure, volume, and temperature all change simultaneously. Temperature must always be in Kelvin (K = °C + 273.15).
