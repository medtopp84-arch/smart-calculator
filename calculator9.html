Hydrostatic Pressure Calculator: P = ρgh in kPa, bar and psi

This calculator finds the pressure at a given depth in a liquid at rest. Enter the depth and choose the fluid, or type your own density. You get the pressure in kilopascals, bar, psi and megapascals, both as gauge pressure and as absolute pressure. If you enter the area of the tank floor or wall, it also gives the force acting on it. It is useful for tanks, pipelines, dams, wells, diving and any design where a column of liquid loads a surface.

What is hydrostatic pressure?

Hydrostatic pressure is the pressure that a fluid at rest exerts because of its weight. The deeper you go, the more fluid is above you, so the pressure grows in direct proportion to the depth. It also grows with the density of the fluid, so a column of brine presses harder than a column of water of the same height.

P = ρ × g × h
Absolute pressure = P + atmospheric pressure (101.325 kPa)
Force on a surface F = P × A

Here P is the gauge pressure in pascals, ρ the fluid density in kg/m³, g the gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²) and h the depth below the free surface in meters. The pressure depends only on the depth and the density, not on the shape or the volume of the container.

Gauge and absolute pressure

The formula P = ρgh gives the gauge pressure, which is the pressure above the atmosphere. Gauges on tanks and pipes usually show this value. The absolute pressure adds the pressure of the air above the liquid, about 101.325 kPa at sea level. Use absolute pressure for gas laws and for cases where the atmosphere is not acting on the other side of the surface.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the depth of the point below the liquid surface in meters.
  2. Select the fluid, or choose Custom and enter the density in kg/m³.
  3. Optionally enter the area of the surface (floor or wall section) in m² to get the force.
  4. Read the pressure in kPa, bar, psi and MPa.

Worked example 1: water tank

The bottom of a tank filled with fresh water to a depth of 10 m.

  • P = 1000 × 9.81 × 10 = 98,100 Pa = 98.1 kPa
  • In bar: 0.981 bar
  • In psi: 98.1 × 0.145038 = 14.23 psi
  • Absolute: 98.1 + 101.325 = 199.4 kPa
  • If the floor area is 20 m², the force is 98.1 × 20 = 1962 kN

Worked example 2: seawater at 30 m

A diver or a pipe at 30 m depth in seawater with a density of 1025 kg/m³.

  • P = 1025 × 9.81 × 30 = 301,657 Pa = 301.7 kPa
  • In bar: 3.02 bar
  • In psi: 43.75 psi

The total pressure on the diver is this plus the atmosphere, roughly 4 bar absolute.

Worked example 3: diesel tank

A diesel storage tank with a liquid level of 6 m and a density of 830 kg/m³.

  • P = 830 × 9.81 × 6 = 48,854 Pa = 48.85 kPa
  • In bar: 0.489 bar
  • In psi: 7.09 psi

Worked example 4: finding the depth

At what depth in fresh water does the gauge pressure reach 3 bar?

  • Rearrange: h = P ÷ (ρ × g) = 300,000 ÷ (1000 × 9.81)
  • h = 30.58 m

A handy rule: about every 10.2 m of fresh water adds 1 bar.

Force on a wall or gate

On a vertical wall, the pressure is zero at the surface and increases linearly with depth, so the load is triangular. For a rectangular wall of width b and liquid height h, the total horizontal force is:

F = ½ × ρ × g × h² × b

and it acts at one third of the height above the bottom. For example, a 3 m high, 5 m wide water wall carries ½ × 1000 × 9.81 × 9 × 5 = 220.7 kN. The calculator's force output uses the pressure at the depth you enter multiplied by the area, which is exact for a horizontal surface such as a tank floor. For a vertical or inclined wall, use the formula above or the average pressure at mid-depth.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring depth from the wrong point. Depth is the vertical distance below the free surface, not the length along a sloped pipe.
  • Mixing gauge and absolute pressure. Check which one your gauge or your formula expects.
  • Wrong density units. Use kg/m³. Water is 1000 kg/m³, not 1 or 1.0 g/cm³ in this field.
  • Using the container volume. A narrow tank and a wide tank with the same depth have the same bottom pressure.
  • Forgetting extra pressure on the surface. A closed pressurized tank adds the gas pressure above the liquid.

Frequently asked questions

How much pressure does 10 meters of water create?

About 98 kPa, close to 1 bar or 14.2 psi, as gauge pressure.

Does pressure depend on the shape of the container?

No. At the same depth in the same fluid, the pressure is the same whether the vessel is wide, narrow or oddly shaped. This is sometimes called the hydrostatic paradox.

How do I convert bar to psi?

Multiply by 14.5038. So 3 bar is 43.5 psi. One atmosphere is 101.325 kPa, or 1.01325 bar.

Is this pressure the same as the pump or line pressure?

No. It is only the static pressure from the column of liquid. Pumps, friction and elevation changes in a pipeline add or remove pressure. See our disclaimer about using results for final design.
